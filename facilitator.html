From Curious to Capable: AI Stages Explained

What to focus on at each stage of the AI readiness journey

Early stage · 0–39%
Building the foundation
The team is at the start of the AI journey. The priority here is not tools — it's trust and shared vocabulary. People need to feel psychologically safe asking "what even is AI?" before they can meaningfully adopt it.
Key actions for the department
Run AI basics sessions
Short, jargon-free workshops on what AI is, what it isn't, and how it relates to day-to-day work. Focus on demystifying, not impressing.
Create safe spaces to ask
Introduce low-stakes forums — a shared chat channel or a lunch Q&A — where any question is welcome. Anxiety drops when curiosity is rewarded.
Surface early champions
Identify 2–3 people already experimenting with AI tools. Let them share small wins. Peer stories land better than management mandates at this stage.
Address anxiety directly
Acknowledge job-security concerns openly. Clarify the organisation's stance on AI replacing vs. augmenting roles before rumours fill the gap.
Signals to watch
Low participation in AI conversationsScepticism or avoidance of AI toolsConfusion about what AI can actually doAny voluntary experimentation = positive signal
Suggested 90-day roadmap
Month 1Run department-wide AI literacy session. Establish a "no silly questions" channel or forum.
Month 2Identify 2–3 champions. Start a monthly "AI in the news" share-out to build exposure without pressure.
Month 3Introduce one low-risk AI tool for a voluntary use case. Collect feedback. Celebrate any small win.
Emerging · 40–59%
From curiosity to capability
There is growing awareness across the department with pockets of real enthusiasm. The challenge now is converting that interest into structured skills — and ensuring the whole team moves forward, not just the early adopters.
Key actions for the department
Structured upskilling
Roll out role-based AI learning paths. Pair abstract concepts with hands-on tasks relevant to each sub-team — not generic AI courses.
Launch pilot programmes
Pick 1–2 defined use cases (e.g. drafting, summarising, data prep). Run structured pilots with clear goals, a defined group, and a feedback loop.
Build an AI community
Formalise what's likely an informal network. A monthly showcase where staff share what they've tried — successes and failures — builds collective intelligence fast.
Bring hesitant staff along
Not everyone is enthusiastic yet. Pair sceptics with champions for job-shadowing. Focus on relevance — show how AI helps with their specific pain points.
Track and celebrate progress
Set visible milestones — completions, experiments tried, hours saved. Recognition at this stage reinforces the momentum and reduces the pull back to old habits.
Set guardrails early
Before enthusiasm outpaces governance, introduce basic AI usage guidelines — especially around data privacy, confidentiality, and when not to use AI.
Signals to watch
Uneven adoption — champions pull ahead, others stallAd-hoc use without guidelines = riskStaff voluntarily sharing AI tips = strong signalPilot uptake above 60%
Suggested 90-day roadmap
Month 1Launch role-specific learning paths. Publish basic AI usage guidelines. Identify pilot use case and participants.
Month 2Run pilot. Hold first community showcase. One-on-one sessions with hesitant team members.
Month 3Review pilot results. Share findings with department. Identify 2nd wave use cases. Re-run awareness survey.
Progressing · 60–74%
From pilots to practice
The department has good momentum and most staff have meaningful AI exposure. The risk at this stage is inconsistency — pockets of strong adoption alongside pockets of resistance. The focus now is consolidation, governance, and handling the sceptics constructively.
Key actions for the department
Consolidate pilots into practice
Convert successful pilots into standard operating procedures. Document what worked, what didn't, and how AI fits into existing workflows.
Build governance frameworks
Formalise AI policies: acceptable use, data handling, human-in-the-loop requirements, and escalation paths. This protects the team and builds trust.
Address sceptics directly
Identify remaining resistance and its root cause — is it skills, trust, or values? Tailor your response. Don't assume one message works for all.
Deepen skills, not just breadth
Move beyond awareness to proficiency. Role-specific advanced training: prompt engineering, AI output evaluation, responsible use judgement.
Set team-level AI goals
Integrate AI adoption into team OKRs or workplans. When AI use is part of how performance is measured, it signals permanence — not a passing initiative.
Share learnings beyond HR
At this stage, HR can become a model for other departments. Document the journey and prepare to present it — it builds HR's credibility as an AI-literate function.
Signals to watch
Isolated pockets still not using AI toolsWorkarounds to avoid AI = underlying concernStaff questioning AI outputs (not just accepting) = maturityCross-team AI knowledge sharing happening organically
Suggested 90-day roadmap
Month 1Document pilot outcomes. Draft governance framework. Map remaining sceptics and root causes.
Month 2Publish governance policy. Launch advanced training. Hold targeted sessions for hesitant groups.
Month 3Embed AI use in team objectives. Prepare an HR AI journey showcase. Re-run awareness survey.
AI-ready · 75–100%
Leading, not just using
The team has strong AI literacy and genuine confidence. The opportunity now is to shift from adopters to advocates — leading responsible AI use across the organisation and setting the standard for how it's done well.
Key actions for the department
Lead organisation-wide
Position HR as the department that other functions look to for AI guidance, governance, and best practices — not just a user, but a standard-setter.
Scale responsible adoption
Expand governance from HR to the broader org. Help other teams navigate the same journey with hard-won lessons already learned.
Drive innovation, not just efficiency
Move beyond "AI saves time" to "AI enables new capabilities." Explore use cases that weren't possible before — predictive analytics, personalised EX, smarter workforce planning.
Stay current — AI moves fast
Build habits for continuous learning: quarterly AI capability reviews, horizon scanning for new tools, and refreshed training as the technology evolves.
Signals to watch
Overconfidence — accepting AI outputs without scrutinyPlateau risk: "we're already AI-ready" complacencyStaff challenging and improving AI workflows proactivelyOther departments asking HR for AI guidance
Suggested 90-day roadmap
Month 1Formalise HR's role as AI centre of excellence. Identify 2 other departments to support on their journey.
Month 2Pilot 1 innovation-tier use case (predictive, generative, or agentic). Conduct horizon-scanning review.
Month 3Present AI maturity journey to senior leadership. Publish revised governance for org-wide use. Re-survey.

groups from 0 participants

The Six Thinking Hats method (Edward de Bono) structures group discussion so everyone thinks from the same perspective at the same time. The 👑 Royal Hat is our addition — the group facilitator. Share this page link with participants so they can read their own hat before the session: hats.html

👑
Royal Hat
Facilitator · 1 per group
  • Guides the group through each hat
  • Manages time per hat
  • Ensures no voice dominates
  • Records commitments at close
SAY:
  • "Let's hear Yellow hat now"
  • "Time to move to Green"
  • "Quick Red hat check before we close"
🤍
White Hat
Facts & Data
  • Focus on data only
  • No opinions or feelings
  • State what is known
  • Identify information gaps
ASK:
  • "What does the data say?"
  • "What facts do we have?"
  • "What information is missing?"
❤️
Red Hat
Emotions & Feelings
  • Share gut reactions freely
  • No justification needed
  • Intuition is valid here
  • Feelings are information
ASK:
  • "How does this make you feel?"
  • "What's your instinct?"
  • "What excites or worries you?"
💛
Yellow Hat
Benefits & Optimism
  • Find the positives
  • Explore best-case outcomes
  • Play the optimist role
  • Look for opportunity
ASK:
  • "What's the best outcome?"
  • "What value does this unlock?"
  • "What has already improved?"
💚
Green Hat
Ideas & Creativity
  • Generate new ideas freely
  • No idea is too wild
  • Challenge assumptions
  • Quantity over quality
ASK:
  • "What if we tried this?"
  • "What if there were no limits?"
  • "What completely new approach works?"
💙
Blue Hat
Process & Planning
  • Focus on structure
  • Define next steps
  • Organise the thinking
  • Opens and closes discussions
ASK:
  • "What is our first step?"
  • "How do we prioritise?"
  • "What is the 30-day plan?"
🖤
Black Hat
Caution & Judgement
  • Identify risks and problems
  • Logical caution — not pessimism
  • Protect against mistakes
  • Ask the hard questions
ASK:
  • "What could go wrong?"
  • "What biases might appear?"
  • "What are we missing?"
Suggested AI readiness debrief sequence (approx 30 min)
👑
Royal — Open (2 min)
  • Share survey results
  • Explain hat sequence
  • Set discussion goal
💙
Blue — Agenda (2 min)
  • Agree on today's focus
  • Set time per hat
🤍
White — Facts (5 min)
  • Review scatter matrix
  • Note quadrant counts
  • State avg score
❤️
Red — Feelings (3 min)
  • One word per person
  • No justification
  • Record the mood
💛
Yellow — Upsides (4 min)
  • What excites us about AI?
  • What's already working?
🖤
Black — Risks (4 min)
  • Name the real concerns
  • Bias, privacy, job fears
💚
Green — Ideas (5 min)
  • What should we try first?
  • Solutions to Black hat risks
💙
Blue — Commit (5 min)
  • One action per person
  • Set next check-in date
